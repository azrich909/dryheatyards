Best drip irrigation systems for Arizona yards

The drip kits, controllers, filters and repair parts that work in Arizona: hard water, high pressure and desert plants that want deep, slow water.

Drip irrigation is the standard for Arizona landscapes because desert plants do best with deep, slow, infrequent watering. A good system is more than a kit: Arizona's hard water clogs emitters and high city pressure blows fittings apart, so a filter and pressure regulator belong on every drip zone.

Arizona drip tips

  • Filter and regulate every zone. Most drip problems in the Valley come from clogged emitters and blown fittings.
  • Water deep, not often. Our desert watering guide covers the 1-2-3 depth rule and run times.
  • Change the schedule with the seasons. A Wi-Fi controller makes this a one-minute job.
  • Check for rebates. Some Arizona cities offer rebates for smart controllers and grass removal. See our rebate guide before you buy.

FAQ

What is the best drip irrigation kit for Arizona?

For most yards, a Rain Bird drip kit plus a Rain Bird filter and pressure regulator is a reliable starting point. The MIXC kit is a lower-cost option for small beds and pots.

Do I need a pressure regulator for drip irrigation?

Yes. Drip parts are designed for low pressure, and city water pressure is often higher, which can pop fittings and emitters off the line. A 25 PSI regulator or a combined filter-regulator solves it.

Should I use a smart controller or a hose timer?

A hose timer is fine for one drip line off a hose bib. For an in-ground system with several zones, a Wi-Fi controller like the Rain Bird ESP-TM2 or Rachio 3 makes seasonal changes much easier.
