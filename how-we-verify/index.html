How we verify contractors

Dry Heat Yards only lists landscaping businesses that hold an active Arizona Registrar of Contractors (ROC) license. Here is our process:

  1. Research. We find Arizona landscaping companies from public sources, including the company's own website and business listings.
  2. License match. We match each business by name or registered DBA against the ROC's official Current Active Contractor Licenses list (most recent file: Nov 2, 2026). Landscape contractors commonly hold a CR-21, R-21 or C-21 license.
  3. No match, no contractor listing. If we can't match a business to an active license, we don't list it as a contractor. Yard maintenance crews are the only exception, and they're labeled separately (see below).
  4. Re-checks. We re-check listings against new ROC files and remove businesses whose licenses lapse.

Yard maintenance crews

In Arizona, a gardener doing only lawn, garden, shrub and tree maintenance doesn't need a contractor license. We list these crews in a separate Yard maintenance only section on city pages. They are not license-verified, and they shouldn't be hired for installs, hardscape, irrigation systems or any job of $1,000 or more. Read our guide

Re-checked every month

Listings aren't a one-time check. Every month we re-check each contractor against the latest Arizona ROC active-license list, test every listed website, and review homeowner reports. Businesses with a lapsed license or that have closed are removed, and each listing shows the month it was last checked. Spot a problem? Report it here.

What "verified" means and doesn't mean

A verified badge means the business matched an active ROC license on the date shown. It does not mean we endorse the company, inspected its work, or checked its insurance. Always get written quotes and check references, and confirm the license on the ROC site before you hire.

Complaints and discipline

A license is a starting point, not a guarantee. Every month we also check our listings against the ROC's Disciplinary Actions list and remove contractors whose license is suspended or revoked. Every licensed profile shows the result: our November 12, 2026 check of the ROC Disciplinary Actions list (511 suspended or revoked licenses) found none of the businesses we list. In November 2026 that check removed one pool company.

Open, settled and disciplined complaint cases are only shown one contractor at a time in the ROC contractor search. Before you hire, search the license number and look at the complaint history. Every licensed listing on Dry Heat Yards has a Check complaints on ROC link for this.

Check a license yourself

  1. Open the ROC contractor search on the official Arizona Registrar of Contractors site.
  2. Search by the license number shown on the listing, or by business name.
  3. Check that the license is active, that the classification fits your job, and whether there are complaints on record.

Licensed, bonded and insured: what it means in Arizona

Contractors often advertise "licensed, bonded and insured." Here is what each part means in Arizona, and what we check.

  • Licensed: the company holds an active Arizona Registrar of Contractors (ROC) license for the type of work. This is what we verify for every contractor we list, and we re-check it monthly. A license is required for most jobs of $1,000 or more.
  • Bonded: every ROC license comes with a license bond. For residential work it is usually $9,000 or $15,000 for general contractors and $4,250 or $7,500 for specialty contractors such as landscapers, depending on the company's yearly volume. Because a bond is required to hold a license, an active ROC license means the company is bonded.
  • Recovery Fund: residential contractors must also pay into Arizona's Residential Contractors' Recovery Fund or post a $200,000 bond. If a licensed contractor damages you by violating contracting law, you may recover up to $30,000 per residence, after first filing against the bond. Hiring an unlicensed contractor means no Recovery Fund protection.
  • Insured: the ROC requires workers' compensation for companies with employees, but it does not require general liability insurance. We don't verify insurance. Before work starts, ask the company for a certificate of insurance and call the insurer to confirm it's current.

Sources: ROC bond information, ROC Recovery Fund, ROC: Before you hire.

Also check before you hire

  • Insurance: ask for proof of liability insurance, and workers' comp if they have employees.
  • References: ask for recent Arizona jobs similar to yours.
  • Written contract: scope of work, materials, price, payment schedule, start and finish dates, and any warranty.
  • Residential jobs: hiring a licensed residential contractor may make you eligible for the ROC's Residential Contractors' Recovery Fund if something goes wrong. Ask the ROC about eligibility.

Who we list

  • Active Arizona ROC license. Every contractor is matched to the Arizona Registrar of Contractors active-license list. We re-check it every month and remove businesses whose license lapses or is suspended.
  • Strong Google reviews. New companies are added only with a Google rating of 4.0 stars or higher from at least 25 reviews. We add up to 10 new companies per city at a time, highest rated first.
  • Any size, same rules. Local owner-operators and larger companies are held to the same standard.
  • Not for sale. No one can pay to be listed or to change their rating.

Companies listed before November 13, 2026 were added under our original rules (an active ROC license) and stay listed as long as their license stays active. Pool companies follow the rules in the Google ratings section below.

Google ratings

The star ratings and review counts you see on Dry Heat Yards come from each business's public Google Business Profile. They are Google's numbers. Dry Heat Yards does not collect, write, edit or remove reviews, and we can't verify what individual reviewers say.

  • Snapshot, not live: each rating is recorded when we add or re-check a listing (currently November 2026) and is updated periodically. The number on Google today may be a little different.
  • Not for sale: businesses can't pay to change their rating. Featured listings show the same Google rating as everyone else.
  • How we use them: to choose which pool companies we list (at least 4.0 stars from 10+ reviews for licensed pool companies; 4.5 from 20+ for weekly pool cleaners), to sort lists by "Highest rated" or "Most reviews," and for the Top Rated award (4.8+ from 100+ reviews).
  • Ratings never replace the license check. A high rating doesn't mean a business is licensed, and a license doesn't mean good work.
  • Read the reviews yourself: search the business name on Google or Google Maps to see the actual reviews and how recent they are.
