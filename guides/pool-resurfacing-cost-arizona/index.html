How much does pool resurfacing cost in Arizona?

Resurfacing an in-ground pool usually costs about $6,000 to $15,000, with a national average around $11,000. Standard plaster is the cheapest lasting finish; pebble costs more but lasts much longer.

What Phoenix-area pricing looks like

Angi's Phoenix cost page puts local resurfacing at about $4.13 per sq ft for plaster, $10.33 per sq ft for pebble and about $1.03 per sq ft for pool paint, with labor around $53 to $63 an hour. Its worked example comes to roughly $6,700 per 1,000 sq ft of surface for a concrete pool. Those Phoenix numbers sit at or below the low end of the national ranges above, so a Valley quote near the bottom of the table is normal, not a red flag by itself.

Phoenix figures from Angi's Phoenix pool resurfacing cost page, checked Nov 16, 2026. Prescott, Flagstaff and other high-country towns have a shorter season and fewer pool crews, so expect quotes there to vary more.

Estimate your pool's surface area

Resurfacing is priced by the pool's interior surface (floor plus walls), not the water surface you see from the deck. A quick estimate:

  1. Floor: length × width.
  2. Walls: the distance around the pool (perimeter) × average depth.
  3. Add the two together.

Example: a 15 × 30 ft pool with an average depth of 5 ft has a 450 sq ft floor and 90 ft × 5 ft = 450 sq ft of wall, so about 900 sq ft. At the Phoenix rates above, that is roughly $3,700 for plaster or $9,300 for pebble, before tile, repairs or start-up. Odd shapes, steps, benches and spas add area, so use the contractor's measurement when you compare bids.

Extra costs to ask about

  • Tile line: replacing the waterline tile at the same time often adds $2,000 – $5,000 or more.
  • Crack and surface repairs: priced separately, often per linear foot.
  • Draining, cleaning and refilling: refilling with a hose is cheap ($40 – $400 in water); trucked water costs much more.
  • Start-up chemicals and brushing: new plaster needs careful water balancing for the first few weeks. Ask whether start-up is included.
  • Permits: some cities require one, especially if you're also changing equipment, plumbing or decking.

Arizona tips

  • Hard water and sun are tough on pool finishes. Valley tap water is very hard (Glendale's water quality report lists about 9 to 17 grains per gallon), and evaporation concentrates those minerals in the pool. Keeping calcium and pH balanced, and partly draining and refilling when calcium gets high, helps any finish last longer.
  • Timing: the pool is empty for several days, and new plaster cures best out of extreme heat. Many owners book for fall through spring, so schedule early.
  • Draining: in Phoenix, the preferred place for a full drain is your home's sanitary sewer cleanout, at no more than about 12 gallons per minute (720 gallons per hour). Draining to the street or alley is only allowed for homes without a cleanout, with a city permit, and never for backwash or saltwater. Chandler and Mesa follow the same cleanout approach. Ask your crew how they will drain, and check your city's rules.
  • Refill timing (Phoenix): refilling in the January to March billing cycles costs less. Phoenix also lets you request a sewer fee review if a refill raises your water use, through MyPHX311, within 60 days of the July bill. Chandler says it does not discount refills.
  • Hydrostatic pressure: an empty pool can shift if groundwater is high. A good contractor knows when to use a relief valve.

How to compare quotes

  1. Compare the same finish and brand (plaster, quartz or pebble), priced for your pool's actual surface area.
  2. Ask what's included: chipping out the old surface, tile, repairs, start-up and refill.
  3. Get the warranty in writing, and ask what voids it (usually water chemistry).
  4. Use an ROC-licensed pool contractor (pool licenses include B-5, KA-5, R-6, CR-6 and C-6) for jobs of $1,000 or more. How to hire a pool company

FAQ

How much does it cost to resurface a pool in Phoenix?

Angi's Phoenix page puts plaster at about $4.13 per sq ft and pebble at about $10.33 per sq ft. A typical 15 by 30 ft pool has roughly 900 sq ft of interior surface, so that is about $3,700 for plaster or $9,300 for pebble before tile, repairs and start-up. Get two or three written quotes.

How do I measure my pool for resurfacing?

Add the floor area (length times width) to the wall area (perimeter times average depth). A 15 by 30 ft pool with a 5 ft average depth is about 900 sq ft. Steps, benches and spas add more.

Where can I drain my pool in Phoenix?

Use your home's sanitary sewer cleanout at no more than about 12 gallons per minute, or reuse the water on salt-tolerant landscaping. Draining to the street or alley is only allowed for homes without a cleanout, with a city permit, and never for backwash or saltwater pools.

When is the best time to resurface a pool in Arizona?

Fall through spring. New plaster cures best out of extreme heat, and in Phoenix refilling during the January to March billing cycles costs less.

Do I need a licensed contractor to resurface my pool?

In Arizona, jobs of $1,000 or more generally require a contractor licensed by the Registrar of Contractors (ROC). Pool licenses include B-5, KA-5, R-6, CR-6 and C-6. Check the license number at roc.az.gov before you sign.
