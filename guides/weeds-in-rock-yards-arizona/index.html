How to stop weeds in your rock yard

Put down a pre-emergent twice a year (fall and late winter) and water it in, pull or spray the weeds that still come up while they're small, and keep at least 2 inches of rock on the ground. Most rock yards that are "always full of weeds" are missing one of those three.

Why Arizona rock yards get so many weeds

  • Two weed seasons. Winter rains bring one crop of weeds. Monsoon storms bring another in summer.
  • Thin rock. Many Valley homes came with a thin layer of rock when they were built, so sunlight still reaches the soil.
  • Blown-in seeds and dust. Wind and storms drop new seeds on top of the rock every year.
  • Drip leaks and overspray. Anywhere that stays damp grows weeds first. Check your drip lines.

Your Arizona weed calendar

WhenWhat to do
Sept – OctPre-emergent #1 for winter weeds. Water it in with about ½ inch of water.
Nov – JanPull or spray winter weeds while they're small, before they go to seed.
Feb – early MarPre-emergent #2 for summer and monsoon weeds, before nights stay above about 55–60°F. Water it in.
July – SeptMonsoon weeds pop up fast after storms. Pull them while the ground is still soft, or spray.

Step 1: Stop weeds before they sprout (pre-emergent)

A pre-emergent doesn't kill weeds you can see. It stops weed seeds from sprouting. Timing is everything: put it down before the weed season, not after.

  • Granules spread with a hand or push spreader are easiest for most homeowners.
  • Water it in. Pre-emergent has to get through the rock to the soil underneath. About ½ inch of water, from rain or a sprinkler, right after you spread it.
  • Follow the label for how much to use, which plants it's safe around, and kids and pets.
  • Don't use it on wood mulch or anywhere you plan to plant seeds soon.

Step 2: Get rid of the weeds already growing

  • Pull them after a rain or a deep watering, when the soil is soft and the roots come out whole. A stand-up weed puller saves your back.
  • Spray them with a weed killer while they're young. Spray on a calm day so it doesn't drift onto plants you want to keep.
  • Get them before they flower. One weed that goes to seed means hundreds more next season.
Careful with "kills everything for a year" products. Some weed killers sold for rock and driveways keep working in the soil for months. Roots from nearby trees and shrubs can reach under the rock, so read the label and keep these well away from anything you want to live.

Step 3: Thicken your rock

Weed tip: Got weeds coming up through your rock? Add 2 inches of new rock right on top of your old rock. The thicker layer blocks sunlight and helps cut down on weeds. Most Valley homes came with a pretty thin layer of rock when they were built. Builders, right? Wink, wink. 😉

Use our free Rock Calculator to see how many tons you need. Kill and clear the weeds first, put down pre-emergent, then add the new rock on top.

Does landscape fabric help?

Sometimes. Fabric under rock slows weeds for a while, but dust and blown-in seeds settle on top of it and weeds root right there. Fabric also tends to work its way up through thin rock and look messy. For most Arizona rock yards, a thick layer of rock plus pre-emergent twice a year does more than fabric alone.
