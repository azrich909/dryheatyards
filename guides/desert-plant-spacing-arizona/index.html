Desert plant spacing chart for Arizona

The simple rule: space plants by their mature width, not the size of the pot. Two of the same plant go one mature width apart (center to center). Keep every plant at least half its mature width from walls, and give spiny plants extra room from walkways.

The 3 spacing rules

  • Plant to plant (center to center): add the two plants' mature widths and divide by 2. For two of the same plant, that's just its mature width.
  • From the house, walls and fences: at least half the mature width. This is the rule University of Arizona Extension and Water - Use It Wisely give for trees and shrubs.
  • From walkways, patios, driveways and pool decks: half the mature width so it won't need constant trimming. For anything with spines or sharp tips (⚠), add about 2 more feet. AMWUA's plant guide says thorny and spiny plants shouldn't go next to walkways, play areas or traffic areas.

Example, red yucca: about 3 ft tall by 5 ft wide. Space them about 5 ft apart, and keep each one at least 2.5 ft from a wall or walkway edge.

Does spacing change by city?

The rules are the same anywhere in Arizona. What changes is which plants do well and how big they get.

  • Low desert (Phoenix metro, Tucson, Yuma, Lake Havasu, Bullhead City): the chart above fits.
  • Mid elevations (Wickenburg, Cottonwood, Sierra Vista, Kingman): many of these still work, but frost-tender plants like bougainvillea and red bird of paradise freeze back. Red yucca does well at low and mid elevations.
  • High country (Prescott, Flagstaff, Show Low): use a local list from your county Extension office, and expect smaller sizes.

Common spacing mistakes

  • Spacing by pot size. A 5-gallon red yucca looks tiny. In a few years it fills a 5 ft circle.
  • Agave and cactus right next to a path. They will poke people, pets and pool floats.
  • Shrubs against the house. They rub stucco, block A/C units and hide pests.
  • Crowding to look full on day one. It means shearing later, which ruins the natural shape. Fill gaps with rock or a few short-lived flowers instead.

FAQ

How far apart should I plant red yucca?

Red yucca grows about 3 feet tall and 5 feet wide, so space plants about 5 feet apart center to center and keep each one at least 2.5 feet from walls, walkways and patio edges. You can go a bit tighter for a fuller look, but expect to thin them later.

How far from my house should I plant a shrub or tree?

At least half of the plant's mature width. A tree that grows 25 feet wide should be planted at least 12.5 feet from the house, walls and fences.

How far should cactus and agave be from a walkway?

Keep spiny plants out of reach of walkways, play areas and pool decks. We suggest half the mature width plus about 2 extra feet.

Does plant spacing change by city in Arizona?

The method is the same everywhere. What changes is which plants grow well and how big they get. The sizes here are for the low desert (Phoenix, Tucson, Yuma, Lake Havasu). In higher areas like Prescott and Flagstaff, many of these plants freeze or stay smaller.
