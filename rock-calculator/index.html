Landscape rock calculator: how many tons do I need?

How to measure your yard for rock

Here's an example yard seen from above. Use the same 3 steps on your own yard.

1

Find your lot size

This example lot is 6,049 sq ft (about 55 × 110 ft). Width × depth gets you close.

Tip: your county assessor's website lists your exact lot size. Search your address there. You can also right-click in Google Maps and choose Measure distance.

3

Divide by 100

2,835 ÷ 100 = about 28 tons of rock at 2 inches deep. That's the number to give the rock yard.

Redoing just the front yard? Measure only the front: length × width, minus the driveway and walk.

Weed tip: Got weeds coming up through your rock? Add 2 inches of new rock right on top of your old rock. The thicker layer blocks sunlight and helps cut down on weeds. Most Valley homes came with a pretty thin layer of rock when they were built. Builders, right? Wink, wink. 😉

How the rock calculator works

It multiplies length by width to get square feet, then by depth to get cubic yards. Most Arizona decorative rock weighs about 1.4 tons per cubic yard. The calculator adds 10% for settling and uneven ground, then rounds up to the nearest half ton, since most rock yards sell in half-ton steps.

How deep should rock be?

  • 2 inches: most front and back yards with ½" or ¾" rock
  • 3 inches: paths, slopes and areas where you want heavier cover
  • 4 inches: larger rock like 1" and up, or for extra weed control

Before you order

  • Check your HOA's approved rock colors and sizes, and get approval in writing.
  • Ask the rock yard about delivery fees and minimums. Delivery is often a flat fee per load.
  • Lay weed barrier or pre-emergent before the rock goes down.
