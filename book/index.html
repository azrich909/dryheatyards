The Arizona Desert Yard Handbook

Most gardening books weren't written for a place that hits 115°F. This one was.

The Arizona Desert Yard Handbook: Plants, Watering, Rock, Tree Trimming, Costs and Hiring the Right Pro gives Phoenix, Tucson and low-desert homeowners plain-English answers, with real numbers instead of general advice. It covers what to plant, how deep to water, how many tons of rock to order, how to trim a palo verde without ruining it, what the work should cost, and how to hire someone who will actually show up. It's about 100 pages, so you can read it in a weekend.

What's inside

  • Drought-tolerant plants and trees that thrive here, with a spacing chart so they don't outgrow their spot, and eight proven trees
  • The 1-2-3 watering rule and seasonal drip schedules, plus a week-by-week plan for new plants
  • A rock calculator that turns your yard's size into tons to order, with a fill-in worksheet
  • Tree and palm trimming done right: no lion-tailing, no topping, and fewer monsoon blow-overs
  • What it really costs: xeriscape conversions, rock, turf removal and pavers, plus city rebates that pay you to remove grass
  • How to hire a licensed pro: check a license in minutes, compare bids, spot the red flags
  • Safety for families and pets: toxic plants, Sonoran Desert toads and bark scorpions
  • Seasonal care: a month-by-month calendar, frost protection, monsoon prep and citrus care
  • Notes for higher-elevation readers in Prescott, Flagstaff, Kingman and Sierra Vista

Whether you just moved to Arizona, bought a new build with a bare dirt backyard, or you're tired of fighting your yard, start here.

About the author

R.D. Moon is a U.S. Air Force veteran with 20 years of service. He lives in Peoria, Arizona, where his own yard is crushed granite, boulders, cactus and pavers, with no lawn to mow. He also runs Dry Heat Yards, so this is our own book.
